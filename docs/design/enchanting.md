# Warlock — Enchanting: affix capacity, magic crystals, and the enchanting recipes

Design document (2026-11-13; #1414, the Enchanting line of the professions epic #1337), a follow-up to `docs/design/professions.md` (#1330) and `docs/design/recipes.md` (#1339). Written against `origin/main` at 926d4d874 (version 0.16.0). Nothing here is implemented; §13 maps it onto implementation issues, one PR each. It builds on what exists: magic items (`docs/dev/magic-items.md`: `Magic` on a `Stack`, `data/affixes.toml` and the 103 files of `data/affixes/`, categories, tiers, the tier window, `loot.rs`), professions and recipes (`docs/dev/crafting.md`: `data/recipes/<skill>/`, the profession screen, the forging wizard, the one check, salvage), parts and gems (`docs/dev/objects.md`), Temper's `Worked` (`magic/worked.rs`), and the spell data (`docs/dev/spells.md`, `data/spells/`). The owner's brief is §2; the owner's two additions of 2026-11-13 (the capacity limit applies to generated loot; how the excess is trimmed) are §4.

**Status: approved by the owner, 2026-11-13.** The sixteen questions of the first draft are answered in §14, with two additions (silver holds magic badly; a crystal may be consumed); every section below is written to the answers. Anything marked "settled" is the owner's own word.

The recipes take their effects from Path of Exile's currency crafting (named once, here); every name, word, and material in the game is this world's own. Every number below is data and hidden in the game; the wiki states it (the owner's rule: the game is vague, the wiki is accurate). Numbers are a first pass; balance beyond it is out of scope. The aim throughout is the owner's: a solo game, short chains, few intermediates, no grinding, time as the only penalty.

## 1. Summary

| Piece | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Capacity | an item holds **1 to 6 affixes in all** (prefixes and suffixes together; the three-per-kind caps stand): 1 for the base, + its quality (Fine +1, Marvellous +2, Masterwork +3: `Worked::improved.len()`), + its main material's **`binding`** (a new `MaterialDef` field, −2 to +2: **silver −2**, soft metals and knapped stone −1, steel and iron 0, gold +1, mithril, adamantium, dragonbone, dragonscale +2), + its set gem (+1, a flawless one +2), + 1 for jewellery (rings and amulets are made to hold magic), clamped to 1..6. `magic::capacity(stack, content)` is the one read. The capacity is **not told to everyone**: the item description gives it only to a craftsperson (Eye for the make) or a character trained in Appraise; the rest are told only, in words, when a recipe finds no room. Only a damaged Masterwork can be over its capacity (nothing is stripped after the fact; the recipes refuse to add to it); every shipped hand-made item is given a make that fits. §3 |
| The loot trim | **settled by the owner**: an item whose affixes outnumber its capacity, **as the very last step of making it** (after its material, gem, rung, and gear condition), drops them one at a time from the kind (prefix or suffix) with more, one of that kind at random, a suffix on a tie, until it fits; the draws come from the item's own stream after its last draw today (the wand rung's), so an item that needs no trim draws exactly as before. **A trimmed rare stays rare** and keeps its name. **Hand-made content is trimmed like loot** (uniques, a legend's `[[item]]`, the heirloom); the shipped ones are given materials, quality, and gems so that none is, and a test holds it. §4, §4.1 |
| Crystals | **six colours**, one stackable object each (`objects/crystal_red.toml`, …, class `materials`): red (blood and fire), blue (mind and frost), green (venom and quickness), white (ward and light), black (grave and shadow), gold (craft and fortune). `data/crystals.toml` names each colour's **tags** (which creatures leave it: the first colour in file order whose tag the creature carries) and **families** (the affix categories it may roll; every category belongs to at least one colour, most to two). Red never rolls mana, blue never rolls life, as the brief asks. A crystal can also be **consumed** (the abilities menu, any character): a little mana, a Willpower mark, a step toward evil. §5 |
| Sources | **Soulglass**, a level-11 necromancy ray (3d6 necrotic, range 6): a living creature it kills leaves no corpse but crystals of its colour (one, two from level 10, three from level 20; a creature of level 15 or more may leave a rare material too). The witch's at level 16; anyone's from its grimoire (the owner's answer). And **salvage**: each affix of an identified magic item gives, on an Enchanting check, one crystal of its colour. §6, §10 |
| The profession | Enchanting gets a recipe directory, `data/recipes/enchanting/`, so its row "Enchanting…" opens its screen; the check is Temper's shape by **Willpower** + Enchanting's `craft` points against the recipe's difficulty + one per three item levels of the roll; **anywhere**, no station, the item unworn and identified; the roll's **item level** is the greater of the item's own and the enchanter's reach (`enchant_level`: 1 at Enchanting 0, 12 at 110). §7 |
| The six recipes | **Kindle** (plain → magic, one affix, 1 crystal), **Quicken** (one more affix on a magic item with room; on a rare it takes a heart-crystal too), **Recast** (a magic item's affixes rolled anew, 1 crystal), **Imbue** (plain → rare, three to six affixes within capacity, 3 crystals, one colour per affix in order), **Ennoble** (magic → rare, one affix more and a name, 1 crystal), **Unbind** (every affix off, back to plain, no crystal). Each an hour or two; a failure spends the crystals and leaves the item as it was; a natural 2 damages it. §8 |
| Seals and rare materials | a **seal** shuts a family out of a roll before it is made: one crystal of the enchantment's colour each, half an hour more, up to three a roll. Two rare materials: the **heart-crystal** (Quicken on a rare: an affix beyond a magic item's two) and the **soul-fetter** (a Recast that keeps one affix chosen). From Soulglass on a creature of level 15 or more (10 %; a dragon always), and very rarely from salvaging a rare of item level 10 or more (3 %). §9 |
| Phases | E1 capacity and the trim, E2 crystals, consuming, and salvage, E3 Soulglass, E4 the profession with Kindle, Quicken, and Unbind, E5 Recast, Imbue, Ennoble, E6 seals and the rare materials; one issue each. §13 |

## 2. The owner's brief, and what exists today

The brief (#1414, 2026-11-13): an item has a **maximum number of affixes**, 1 to 6, from its quality (plain/Fine/Marvellous/Masterwork) + its material + its pommel or ornament (the set gem, #1356, and other parts); **magic crystals** as Enchanting's material, from **salvaging magic items** and from a **necromancy spell** that kills a living enemy and turns its body into a crystal; the **enemy type determines the crystal's type**; a crystal's **colour limits what can roll** (red cannot roll mana, blue cannot roll health); the **recipes follow Path of Exile's currency crafting** without orbs (plain → magic with one affix; add one affix to a magic item with room; reroll a magic item's affixes; plain → rare with several; magic → rare adding one; strip all back to plain) plus **banning affix types** before a roll at a cost; **rare crafting materials** from draining powerful foes or very rarely from salvaging powerful gear (one that adds an extra affix beyond the normal roll, one that locks an affix). The owner's comment: the capacity limit applies to **generated loot** too, the excess trimmed; and (answers of 2026-11-13) how: one at a time from the kind with more affixes, at random within it, a suffix on a tie, a trimmed rare staying rare.

## 3. Affix capacity: how many enchantments an item holds

**Decision: capacity counts every affix, prefix and suffix alike, 1 to 6; it is the sum of four terms read from state that exists, plus one new material number.**

| Term | Value | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| The base | 1 | every item with a `kind` |
| Jewellery | +1 | `ObjectDef::is_jewellery()` (ring and amulet kinds): a ring is made to carry a charm, and loot already gives jewellery most of the stat and skill suffixes |
| Quality | plain 0, **Fine +1, Marvellous +2, Masterwork +3** | `Worked::improved.len()` (so a damaged item, whose improvements are none while damaged, counts as plain) |
| Material | its main part's **`binding`**, −2 to +2 (the table below) | `MaterialDef::binding`, a new optional field (default 0, steel's), of the main part's material as `Stack::parts` resolves it (a bronze long sword's bronze; the base material otherwise) |
| The set gem | **+1**; a **flawless** cut +2 | the ornament part among `Stack::parts` whose material is a gem (`ornament_part`, Set's rule), by its `GemCut::cut` |
| Clamp | at least 1, at most 6 | `MAX_PER_KIND × 2` |

`binding`, a first pass over the 56 materials (the wiki's materials table gains the column; the owner set silver apart at −2):

| `binding` | Materials | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **−2** | **silver** | the owner's: silver holds magic badly. It is meant to carry anti-magic properties of its own later (a future line, not designed here: nothing in this document reads silver beyond its `binding`) |
| −1 | tin, pewter, zinc, cadmium, bendalloy, lead; stone, flint, obsidian | soft or dull stuff that will not hold a charm: a tin ring is a tin ring |
| 0 | iron, steel, copper, bronze, brass, aluminium, duralumin, chromium, nicrosil; wood, hardwood; bone, horn, chitin; leather, hardened leather, fur; cloth, wool; glass; smokestone and the other gems (never a main part but the loose gem's) | the common stuff |
| +1 | gold, electrum; ironwood; ivory; jade, amber; silk | the fine and the precious: what the old charms were made of |
| +2 | mithril, adamantium; dragonbone, dragonscale | the legendary stuff; also the rarest, so a rare of six affixes stays a deep find or a master's work |

What that gives, with the clamp (the forge's grades from `recipes.md` §4; loot's gear condition rolls Fine on 4 % of creature gear and Masterwork on 0.2 %):

Rules around it:

| Rule | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Prefixes only, or all?** | **all affixes together** (the owner's answer 1); `MAX_PER_KIND` (3 of each) stands beside it | the brief says "prefixes (affixes)"; counting prefixes alone would let a plain sword carry three suffixes beside its one prefix, and the owner's "1 to 6" is exactly two kinds × three |
| **Jewellery has no grade** | rings and amulets have no `Aspect`, so no Fine; their +1 for the kind and the precious metals and gems carry them to 5 | a gold-and-gem ring at 4 is the natural rare base, which matches what loot already makes of jewellery |
| **Over capacity** | after the trim, only one thing leaves an item over its capacity: a **damaged** Fine, Marvellous, or Masterwork piece (its improvements are none while damaged, so its capacity falls under its affixes). **Nothing is stripped after the fact**: the affixes work as they do today. The recipes that add (Kindle, Quicken, Imbue, Ennoble) refuse an item at or over its capacity ("It holds all it can."); Recast and Unbind work on it as on any (Recast keeps the count the item has, so a damaged Masterwork rare rerolls to its old count). A unique, a legend's item, or the heirloom is **never** over: §4.1 gives each shipped one a make that fits, and a test holds it | stripping on damage would be a hit-time rule on every item, for a corner; refusing to add is one check in one place |
| **Repair does not raise it again** | `Worked` keeps nothing of a damaged item's improvements: mended by Temper, it is plain. So a damaged Masterwork rare, repaired, is a plain item over its capacity, and stays so until Temper improves it again | `worked.rs`'s rule; the capacity design changes nothing in it |
| **Ammunition** | a stack's capacity is its one item's; the whole stack is one `Magic`, so **a whole stack is enchanted at once, whatever its size, at the single-item cost** (the owner's answer 7): one crystal binds forty arrows | as today; no per-arrow state |
| **The one read** | `magic::capacity(stack, content) -> u8` in a new `magic/capacity.rs`, beside `Numbers`; `Magic::check` **does not** refuse an item over capacity (saves must load, and a damaged item is valid); a shipped hand-made item is held to its capacity by a **test**, not by the loader (§4.1) | one place; a save never fails to load because of a repair |
| **What the player sees** | **not told by default** (the owner's answer 2). The capacity line is added to the item description (`describe::lines`, a `LineKind::Stat` after the affixes) **only for a holder of Eye for the make** (the craftsperson's passive, `docs/design/craftsperson.md` §7.2: one more line in that talent's read, `TalentMods::eye_for_make`) **or a character trained in Appraise at all** (any value above zero in the skill: the simplest rule, one read, no threshold to tune; the craftsperson always has Appraise, so the talent mostly names a claim the skill already makes, and keeps it if a variant of the class lacks the skill). Words: "It can bear two enchantments." for a plain item, "It bears one enchantment and could bear one more." for a magic one, "It bears all it can." at capacity, "It bears more than its make should hold." when a damaged one is over. Everyone else is **never** told the number or the line; a recipe that refuses for lack of room says so in words ("It holds all it can.", "It would not hold so much.", the row greyed "it holds all it can"), which is all an untrained enchanter learns. The lists' `Lore::listed` numbers do not change. The dumps (`inventory`, `actors`) show the truth: they are dev surfaces |

## 4. The trim: capacity binds generated loot and hand-made items alike (settled)

**The owner's decisions (2026-11-13), written as settled:** when an item is made with more affixes than its base supports, the excess is trimmed, one affix at a time; a trimmed rare stays rare; the trim is **the very last step of making an item**; and hand-made content is **not exempt** (it is given the materials its affixes need: §4.1).

| Rule | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Where** | **the very last step** of making an item, after the material (`variant::draw`), the ornament (`gem::ornament`), the wand's rung, **and gear condition** (`world/gear_condition.rs`), so the capacity is read from the item exactly as it will exist. `loot::make_from` stops short of it; the one function `loot::finished(content, rng, stack)` (the trim, `trim(magic, capacity, rng)` in `magic/capacity.rs`) is the last line of every path that completes an item: `make`, `make_as`, `roll_with`, `roll_pool`, the heirloom, and creature gear (`gear.rs` calls it **after** `gear_condition` has drawn). Callers: floor loot, chests, hoards, dens, room loot, creature gear, animated things, starting kits (`kit.rs`), the background's gifts, the stipend, the pedlar's offer (`favoured`), the heirloom, and the balance probe. A path that forgets the call is caught by a test that rolls every entry point at a deep level and asserts `count <= capacity` |
| **Which affixes go** | repeat until the count fits the capacity: take the **kind (prefix or suffix) with more affixes** on the item; drop **one of that kind at random**; on a **tie, drop a suffix**. So a four-affix rare of two and two on a capacity-1 base loses a suffix, then (2 against 1) a prefix, then (1 against 1) a suffix: a prefix is left |
| **The draws** | each random drop is one `rng.index(n)` among that kind's affixes **from the same stream the item rolled from**, after its last draw today (the wand rung's, or gear condition's if that path draws it from the same stream; the implementer reads `gear_condition.rs` for which stream it uses and keeps the trim on the item's own). An item that needs no trim draws exactly as before; one that does shifts what follows on that stream (the next find of a hoard, the next piece of a creature's gear). Those shifts move the mapgen snapshots that hold magic items and the replays through them; the PR regenerates and notes them (`docs/dev/merging.md`: generated files are taken whole). No new stream: a derived one per item would need a key per call site |
| **Rarity** | **unchanged**: a rare trimmed to two affixes, or one, is a rare still, with its name and its colour; `Magic::check` already allows it (at least one affix, at most three of a kind). A magic item is never trimmed (at most two affixes, a prefix and a suffix; a capacity-1 base drops its suffix, so a plain tin ring is magic with one affix) |
| **Gear condition** | `world/gear_condition.rs` draws Fine, Marvellous, or Masterwork **after** the gear is made, which can only raise the capacity, and a damaged condition empties the improvements, which lowers it. The owner's order (the trim last) makes both count: a Fine piece keeps a second affix it rolled (its capacity is 2 once the condition is drawn), and a damaged piece is trimmed to its plain capacity. Gear's draws before the trim are unchanged; only the trim's are appended |
| **Hand-made content** | **not exempt** (the owner's answer 3): a unique's template (`content/unique.rs`), a legend's `[[item]]` (`level.rs::magic_of`), and the heirloom (`loot::heirloom`, Father's sword) go through the same trim. Uniques and legend items are made at load, where there is no item stream, so they use the same `trim` with a stream keyed by the item's own id (`trim/{id}`), a backstop that never fires on shipped data. **The shipped items are instead given the quality, material, gem, or jewellery their affixes need** (§4.1), and a dev test, `hand_made_items_are_never_trimmed`, runs every unique, every legend `[[item]]` of every campaign and the showcase, and the heirloom (every race's weapon at its top count), asserts that the capacity holds all their affixes, and fails with the item's id and the shortfall. Quality is a data key hand-made items did not have: `quality = "fine" \| "marvellous" \| "masterwork"` on a unique, a legend's `[[item]]`, and the heirloom effect (it fills `Worked::improved`, as the forge's grade does), beside the `parts` key that already sets materials and gems (E1) |
| **Value** | an item's worth follows its affixes (`value_with` and the rarity multipliers): a trimmed rare is worth less than it rolled. No compensation in `[rarity]`: §12 measures the effect, and the weights are tuned after |

The order is the deliberate one: a creature's craft (caster, priest) before its tribe, its tribe before its role (`archer`). Gold's living sources are few on purpose (the fey, and the summoners who call them): gold crystals come mostly from salvaging the fortune suffixes (of Haggling, of Appraisal, …), which loot puts on jewellery.

## 9. Seals and the rare materials

**Decision: a seal costs a crystal of the colour used and half an hour; two rare materials, each one recipe's input, from the powerful dead and very rarely from salvaged rares.**

| Piece | Design |
|---|---|
| **A seal** (the brief's "banning affix types") | before a roll (Kindle, Quicken, Recast, Imbue, Ennoble), the wizard's optional steps let the enchanter shut **up to three families** of the crystal's colour (Imbue: of the union) out of the roll: `CraftStepKind::Family`, a paged list of the colour's families in the words the description uses ("fire", "the critical", "mana", "stealth"; `data/crystals.toml` `[[family]] { category = "fire_damage", word = "fire" }` names each category once, checked complete at load), picked by letter, `-` for none. **Each seal costs one more crystal of the same colour** (so a Kindle with two seals takes three red crystals; Imbue's seals cost a crystal of any of its three colours, the first that fits) and adds **30 minutes**. The seals hold for that one roll only (no state on the item). The dialog names them. A seal on a family the item already has, or the base cannot take, is refused ("There is nothing to seal there.") |
| Why crystals and not gold or a rare material | the brief says "at a cost"; a crystal is the one currency the profession has, it is plentiful (§12), and it keeps the loop closed (no shop sells crystals, no shop buys seals). PoE's bench fee is currency too (the owner's answer 9) |
| **The heart-crystal** (`objects/heart_crystal.toml`: class `materials`, 30 g, value 300, glyph `*` crimson, `materials = ["stone"]`, stackable) | the brief's "adds an extra affix beyond the normal roll": **Quicken on a rare** takes one beside its crystal (a magic item's Quicken needs none: its second affix is a magic item's due). So a rare grows past Imbue's roll one affix at a time, within its capacity, each costing a heart-crystal and a crystal of the colour wanted |
| **The soul-fetter** (`objects/soul_fetter.toml`: class `materials`, 30 g, value 300, glyph `*` pale, `materials = ["stone"]`) | the brief's "locks one": **Recast with a soul-fetter** keeps one affix the enchanter chooses (the wizard's last step, over the item's known affixes) and rerolls the rest; spent made or failed |
| Sources | **Soulglass on a creature of level 15 or more**: 10 % one rare material (even odds heart-crystal or soul-fetter), a **dragon** (the `dragon` tag) always a heart-crystal (`data/crystals.toml` `[rare] level = 15, chance = 10, dragon = "heart_crystal"`); **salvaging a rare item of item level 10 or more**: 3 % one (§10). Never in shops, never floor loot. No cap (the house rule: time is the penalty; a level-15 foe is hours of play) |
| A third? | PoE's reroll of values within tiers (a "polish") is left out: the tier window already keeps values close, and a third rare object is a third thing to explain (the owner's answer 10: two) |

## 10. Salvage changes: crystals from magic items

**Decision: salvage keeps its checks and adds one per affix, on Enchanting, before the materials'; a crystal per affix made; unidentified magic gives nothing.**

| Rule | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| When | `world/salvage.rs`, after the stack is set apart and before the material checks: for an **identified** magic or rare item, one check per affix, in the item's affix order | the professions design's "Enchanting's hook on salvage's spot" |
| The check | 2d12 + Willpower's modifier + `TalentMods::check(willpower)` + `skill_effect_of(enchanting, Craft)` against `data/enchanting.toml` `salvage_difficulty = 12` + the affix's tier ÷ 3 (a top tier +4); two gameplay draws each, all drawn before anything changes, as the material checks are | the item's deeper magic is harder to catch |
| Made | **one crystal of the affix's colour** (`colour_of_affix`: the first colour holding its category) into the pack: "You catch the Sharp of it in a red crystal." Then the material checks run as today and the item is gone | one affix, one crystal: Kindle's price, so a magic item salvaged and another kindled is a trade of hours, not a gain |
| Failed | nothing: "The magic goes out of the blade unread." | |
| A rare of item level 10 or more | after its affixes, one chance draw: 3 % a rare material (§9) | the brief's "very rarely from salvaging powerful gear" |
| Unidentified | its affixes give nothing and are never named ("Its magic goes out unread."); no draws for them | a crystal's colour would tell the affix's family: a leak around identification. The hour of Enchanting's `identify_magic`, the scroll, or the spell comes first |
| Untrained | the checks are made at Willpower alone, as salvage's others are for the untrained; the crystals are theirs to sell (value 25) or keep | salvage's one rule; the recipes stay the trained's |
| Marks | `Use::Salvage` on Enchanting when any affix check was made (the existing filter: that skill alone), 100 % | every check is practice |
| Refusals | unchanged (worn, protected, unpaid, a spirit weapon, nothing a profession works): an item whose only worth is its affixes (a wool ring?) still has a material or is refused as today | |
| Words | the saved list: "You save 2 steel ingots, a red crystal, and a blue crystal." | |

Unbind (§8) gives no crystal: a crystal comes of breaking a thing or a body, not of a clean unbinding; else Unbind + Kindle would recolour an item for nothing but hours (the owner's answer 8: nothing back).

## 11. What the player sees; determinism; saves; the wiki and the docs

## 12. Balance against found loot

Today magic loot is 3.25 % of finds and rares 0.5 %; a rare has three to six affixes on any base. After this design:

| Effect | Estimate | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Rares are trimmed** | loot's bases are plain and mostly common metals: a rare on a plain steel sword (capacity 1) keeps one affix; jewellery (capacity 2 to 4: iron 2, gold 3, gem-set gold 4; silver 1, or 2 with a flawless gem), gem-set weapons (2: the `[[ornament]]` chance is a few percent of a class's loot), and mithril or dragonbone variants (3) keep more. The shares test measures it; the first-pass expectation is that about a third of rolled rares keep three or more affixes, nearly all of them jewellery. Found rares get rarer in effect; found magic items are untouched (two affixes fit any base but a tin ring or a flint knife) | this is the owner's intent: a deep rare is a deep base. The player who wants six affixes makes the base (a Masterwork mithril blade, a flawless gem) or finds it: the gates are Metalcraft, Jewelcraft, and depth, not Enchanting |
| **Crystals are plentiful** | Soulglass costs about 11 mana a cast (level 11, a ray, `attack`) and a kill gives one to three crystals; a floor of twenty living kills is ten to twenty crystals; salvage adds one per affix caught. A Kindle costs one, an Imbue three, a seal one more | as PoE's common currency; the limit is the capacity and the roll, not the crystal. Numbers to tune by the yield levels (`[yield] levels = [10, 20]`) and the salvage difficulty |
| **Capacity is the gate** | an Imbue needs a capacity-3 base: a Marvellous anything, a Fine item with a gem, a gold ring, a Fine mithril or ironwood piece; six needs a Masterwork and a flawless gem or dragon stuff. Masterwork from the forge is a master's one in four (`recipes.md` §4), Temper's third improvement is at difficulty 17 | the professions chain together: the smith makes the base, the jeweller the gem, the enchanter the magic; a solo character trains two of the three over a game |
| **Rolls at the reach** | a level-5 wizard with Enchanting 30 rolls at item level 4: +1 to +2 affixes, the first tiers; the top tiers at Enchanting 110, which the level-up rolls reach past level 20 | the enchanter grows as loot's depth does; a deep found rare (level 12) keeps its depth under any enchanter |
| **Consuming** | a crystal drunk is 6 mana, a Willpower mark, and −20 alignment: a Soulglass cast (about 11 mana) that kills pays back 6 to 18 mana at one to three crystals, but only by spending the crystals the enchanter wanted, and every one moves the soul toward evil (the owner's price). Watch it in the balance probe's mana rows; tune `[consume] mana` first | the owner's secondary use; not a loop that needs no foe |
| **Rare materials are scarce** | a level-15 foe gives one in ten; a dragon one; a salvaged deep rare one in thirty-three | a rare's fifth and sixth affix and a kept affix on a Recast are the long game's prizes |
| **Against the forge and Temper** | a crafted Masterwork is `finished` (no Temper), so its capacity is 4 bare and 6 with a flawless gem: the forge's best work is the enchanter's best base, which is where the owner's chain points | |
| **Where to watch** | the balance probe (`cargo test --release -p warlock-core balance_probe`) gains a row: a level-20 character's self-made rare against found loot of the depth; `docs/balance.md` records it | |

No change to `[rarity]` or `[rare] affixes` in this design: the trim's effect is measured first (the shares test, the probe), and the weights are tuned after, by an issue of their own.

## 14. Owner's answers (2026-11-13)

The owner approved the design with these answers to the sixteen questions of the first draft, and two additions (17 and 18). Each question, then its answer.

1. **Capacity counts every affix, or prefixes only?** **All affixes**, prefixes and suffixes together, with the three-per-kind caps beside it.
2. **Is the capacity shown in the item description?** **Not by default.** It is told to a craftsperson (through Eye for the make) and to a character **trained in Appraise** (any value above zero: the simplest rule, one read); everyone else is never told, and a recipe that refuses for lack of room says so in words (§3).
3. **Is hand-made content exempt from the trim?** **No.** Uniques, legend items, and the heirloom are trimmed like loot; **the shipped hand-made items are given appropriate materials** so their capacity fits their affixes (§4.1 lists each and its change), and a dev test checks that none is trimmed.
4. **Creature gear: trim before or after its condition?** The trim is **the very last step** of making an item, after gear condition and everything else (§4).
5. **Willpower or Intelligence?** **Willpower.**
6. **Where may a player enchant?** **Anywhere.**
7. **Ammunition?** A **whole stack is enchanted at once**, whatever its size, at the single-item cost.
8. **Does Unbind give anything back?** **Nothing.**
9. **What does a seal cost?** One crystal of the colour and half an hour, up to three a roll.
10. **Rare materials?** **Two.**
11. **Who gets Soulglass?** The **witch's at 16 and a grimoire** for anyone.
12. **Yields?** As written (one, two from level 10, three from 20; rare materials from level 15 at 10 %, a dragon always).
13. **How many colours?** **Six**, with the tag order black, blue, white, red, green, gold.
14. **Jewellery's capacity?** **+1.**
15. **`binding`: a new material field?** **A new field.**
16. **`[rarity]` and `[rare] affixes`?** **Measure first**; no change in this design.
17. **Silver (added).** **Silver is a special material with `binding` −2**: it holds magic badly, and will later get anti-magic properties (a future line, not designed here). Every table and example that gave silver +1 is changed (§3; the silver ring's capacity is 1, or 2 with a flawless gem).
18. **Consuming a crystal (added).** A magic crystal has a secondary use: it can be **consumed**, to refill mana, train Willpower, and shift alignment toward evil. Designed in §5 ("Consuming a crystal"): the abilities menu's Consume, `consume:LETTER`, a flat 6 mana, one Willpower mark, −20 alignment a crystal, all data; built in E2.
