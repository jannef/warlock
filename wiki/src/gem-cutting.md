# Gem cutting

A loose [gem](gems.md) can be cut to a finer [cut](gems.md#size-and-cut), which makes it worth
more. Cutting is **Cut**, a [recipe](skill-uses.md#professions) of
[Jewelcraft](skills.md#skill-jewelcraft). You need to be trained in Jewelcraft: open
[Jewelcraft…](abilities.md#ability-jewelcraft) in the [abilities menu](abilities.md#the-abilities-menu),
choose Cut, then the gem. You need no tools and can cut anywhere. It takes half an hour. If
something comes into view, you stop, the gem is untouched, and the time is lost.

## What can be cut

- Any **uncut** gem, whatever its size.
- A gem already cut, to cut it again (a **re-cut**), unless it is **tiny** ("There is nothing left
  to cut.") or already **flawless** ("It could not be finer.").

One gem of a stack is cut; the rest stay as they were.

## How well it comes out

When the time is up you make one check: 2d12, plus your
[Dexterity](stats.md#stat-dexterity) [modifier](stats.md#modifiers), the
[craft points](crafting.md#which-skill-makes-what) Jewelcraft gives (+8 at 100, +12 at 200), and your
[talents](talents.md) for Dexterity checks. The difficulty is 12 plus the gem's
[work](crafting.md#which-skill-makes-what): a ruby is harder to cut than a smokestone. In
[Gimmwīc](ascmearc.md#gimmwīc), once the [crystal guardian](quests.md#lures) stands bound there,
you get **+2** more. How far you beat it (the **margin**) sets the cut:

## Losing stone

Cutting takes stone. On an uncut gem, a failure ("The stone splits badly; what is left is
smaller.") or a crude cut leaves it one [size](gems.md#size-and-cut) smaller. A fair cut or better
keeps its size. A tiny stone has no size to lose: a failure or a crude cut crumbles it, and
nothing worth keeping is left.

A re-cut always costs a size, and it never makes the cut worse: if your check does no better than
the cut the gem has, it keeps that cut. So a re-cut pays on a large crude stone, and seldom
otherwise. Compare the worth in the table above.

A natural 2 shatters the gem, whatever its size.

## Setting a gem

**Set**, the other gem recipe on [Jewelcraft…](abilities.md#ability-jewelcraft), puts a cut gem
into an item you already have: a sword's pommel, a shield's or bow's inlay, a helmet's studs, a
ring's or an amulet's setting. You need Jewelcraft 10. Choose the item, then the gem; you are
asked to confirm, with the item named as it will be: "Set a sapphire-pommelled hand axe?"

- The item must have a place for a gem with none in it yet ("It is set already."). Something you
  wear or wield must come off first.
- Only a cut gem can be set, never an uncut one.

It takes 20 minutes, anywhere. If something comes into view, you stop, nothing is lost, and the
time is gone. Then you make one check, as for cutting: 2d12, plus your Dexterity modifier, the
craft points of Jewelcraft, your talents for Dexterity checks, and the crystal guardian's +2 in
Gimmwīc, against 12 plus the gem's work.

- **Made:** the gem goes in with its size and cut, and the item is named by it ("It is now a
  ruby-pommelled long sword."). A set gem adds its worth to the item and does nothing else.
- **Failed:** the item is untouched, and the gem chips: its cut drops one (down to crude), and if
  it cannot, its size drops one. A tiny crude gem has nothing left to lose and is gone.
- **A natural 2** shatters the gem.

check, and it is never lost.

## Training

Every cut or setting you finish may train [Jewelcraft](skills.md#improving-a-skill), whatever came of
it.
