# Classes

Your class is the last choice of a new run (see [Your character](character.md)): what you did
before you set out. For now a class changes your starting [stats](stats.md), adds to your hit
points (see [Combat](combat.md#hit-points)), trains you in a handful of
[skills](skills.md#who-trains-what) (and sets how many skill rolls each level gives and which
skills it is known for, see [Getting better](skills.md#getting-better)), and gives you your first ranks in a few
[weapon skills](weapon-skills.md) (a barbarian has learned one of two) and
[fighting styles](fighting-styles.md) (a thief or a monk one of two), and decides most of your
[starting kit](starting-kit.md); no spells come with it. A class with a level table also gives
**class abilities** as you reach its levels, and bonus [talents](talents.md) (see
[Levels](#levels) below).

The first eleven are adventurers. The last four are everyday folk: an aristocrat, a commoner, a
craftsperson, and a merchant, who were never trained for this and mostly show it.

The changes are added to the average score of 10, together with your race's, your gender's, and
your [birth sign's](horoscope.md). Each class rolls dice for its key stats, and sometimes for a
weakness too: `1d3+1` is two to four, `−1d2` takes away one or two (see
[How your stats add up](character.md#how-your-stats-add-up)). Classes are not balanced against
each other, and some are poor choices on purpose.

## Levels

Each class's level table gives an ability at levels 1, 6, 12, 18, 25, 32, 40, 50, and 60, and
a bonus talent at a few levels between, from the trees it names. An ability comes the moment
you reach its level ("You gain Fencing."), and works from then on; in the game each is told in
words, here exactly. A stat it raises rises for good, score and potential. Some abilities are
used from the [abilities menu](abilities.md); the rest simply work. So far the warrior, the
barbarian, the paladin, the monk, the priest, the druid, the wizard, the witch, the ranger, the
thief, the bard, and the everyday folk have their tables. A table may hand out an ordinary talent
(the ranger's and the thief's Evasion, the bard's Lore); if you took it already, the level gives
nothing in its place.

A paladin's abilities used from the menu answer only while the paladin is Good or better (see
[Alignment](alignment.md)); the passives do not ask. Lay on hands drains you instead of costing
mana. The barbarian's rage runs on a pool of its own, Rage, and the
ranger's rapid shot and volley on [focus](resources.md) (see [Resources](resources.md)); the
thief's sneak attacks build [openings](resources.md), which master thief's finisher spends; the
monk's flurry and quivering palm run on [ki](resources.md), and the bard's inspire spends
[inspiration](resources.md) when the bard has enough.

The spells a class gives (the priest's, druid's, wizard's, and witch's few, a bard's, a
paladin's, or a ranger's one or two; see [What your class gives](magic.md#what-your-class-gives))
are used from the menu as **spells** ([Magic](magic.md)): each costs mana by its tier, your caster level, and your armour, and is
greyed out ("too little") while you have less; there is no other wait. A burst flies and goes
off as a [scroll](scrolls.md)'s does, a line runs through everyone on it, its blows and kills
yours; a hex falls on a hostile you see within its reach. Call lightning answers only under the
open sky. A priest's
Smite is the paladin's, and answers only while the priest is Good or better.

The ranger's **favoured prey** is the kind of creature you have killed most, counted over every
level; when two are tied, the one that got there first stays favoured until another is killed
more often. A **volley** looses three shots of your readied missile weapon at the place you aim
at, one after another, for the time of one ordinary shot; it stops early when you run out, and
costs 6 focus. **Rapid shot** gives you focus (10, full); every shot you loose while you have
at least 2 spends 2 and takes 10 % less time, and with less it is an ordinary shot. The bard's
**inspire** costs 3 inspiration when you hold 3, else 3 mana, and can be sung again while it
lasts (it starts over); a virtuoso's costs nothing at all, and keeps the inspiration.

The monk's **flurry** gives you ki (5, full): every attack with nothing in your hands strikes a
second blow, at −2 to hit, for a point of ki; with none left, the attack is a single blow.
**Quivering palm** costs all 5 ki and has no other wait.
