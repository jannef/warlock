# Using your skills

INTRO

## Slipping past

Most [creatures](creatures.md) see a few cells in any darkness ([darkvision](vision.md#the-four-types)), so standing
unlit beside one is no hiding place by itself. But the first time a creature that is not hunting
you would see you **only** by its darkvision (your cell too dark for its eyes: no
[lantern](lantern.md), no fire, no sky lighting you), it does not notice you at once: you try to
slip past it, once. It is a contest, the same dice on both sides: 2d12 plus your
[Dexterity](stats.md#stat-dexterity) [modifier](stats.md#modifiers) plus what your
[Stealth](skills.md#skill-stealth) gives (the benefits table above), against 2d12 plus the
creature's [Willpower](stats.md#stat-willpower) modifier plus its [Spot](skills.md#skill-spot), if it has any. A
tie is yours; a roll of 2 always fails and a roll of 24 always works. Your **edge** is the
difference:

{{#include generated/slipping-past.md}}

- **Unseen**, it does not see you while you stay in the dark: it goes about its business, walks
  past, or comes to look into the [footsteps](hearing.md#what-makes-noise) it heard and arrives beside you none the
  wiser. It has not seen you, so your first blow at it is a
  [sneak attack](skills.md#skill-sneak-attack). It notices you when you step into light it can
  see by (or light your [lantern](lantern.md)), when you attack it or anything else while it can
  see you, when it is hurt, or when the time above has passed.
- **Seen**, it hunts you as any creature would, and you get no second try until it gives up
  the hunt.

Nothing tells you which it was: a creature that did not notice you gives no sign.
Creatures never slip past you.

## Stealing

If you are trained in **[Steal](skills.md#skill-steal)** ([thieves](classes.md#thief) are), **[Steal](abilities.md#ability-steal)** is in your
[abilities menu](abilities.md): choose it, then a direction, and you try to lift something from
whoever stands there. You can steal only from someone you see who is not hostile to you: a
peaceful villager, a shopkeeper, a [creature](creatures.md) that leaves you alone. It takes a turn whatever comes
of it.

Whether you go unnoticed is a contest, the same dice on both sides: 2d12 plus your [Dexterity](stats.md#stat-dexterity)
[modifier](stats.md#modifiers) plus what your [Steal](skills.md#skill-steal) skill gives (the benefits table above), against 2d12 plus your
victim's [Willpower](stats.md#stat-willpower) modifier. A tie is yours; a roll of 2 always fails and a roll of 24 always
works. Only the difference between the two sides matters, your **edge**:

{{#include generated/stealing.md}}

- **Unnoticed**, you take one stack of what they carry in their pack, all of it: a whole purse.
  You never see what someone carries in their pack, so you do not choose: your fingers find
  something. What they wear or hold in their hands stays where it is. With nothing in their
  pack you come away empty-handed ("You find nothing to take from the gardener."), and they
  notice nothing all the same.
- **Caught**, they turn on you and come after you, for good ("The kobold catches you stealing and
  turns on you!"). A shopkeeper who catches you is outraged as a robbed one is: the [shop](shops.md) trades
  no more, and whatever you had not paid for is stolen (see [Shops](shops.md)).

Stealing from a shopkeeper takes from their purse, never from the [shop](shops.md) floor: what lies in a [shop](shops.md)
is for sale, and taking it out without paying is theft by the shop's own rules. A shopkeeper
whose purse you lifted unnoticed still trades with you, but cannot pay for what you sell.

You may try as often as you like; someone who caught you stays angry, and that is the price.

## Feinting

If you are trained in **[Bluff](skills.md#skill-bluff)**, **[Feint](abilities.md#ability-feint)** is in your
[abilities menu](abilities.md): choose it, then a direction, and you make a lying move at whoever stands
there. It must be a [creature](creatures.md) you see, hostile to you, with a mind to fool: not a skeleton, a spirit, or
an animated thing (those without [vitals](combat.md#criticals)). Otherwise it is refused and takes no time. A feint
takes a turn.

The foe rolls 2d12 plus its [Willpower](stats.md#stat-willpower) [modifier](stats.md#modifiers) against the difficulty
below plus your [Presence](stats.md#stat-presence) modifier plus what your Bluff adds (the benefits table above). Short
of it, "The kobold falls for it.": it is [surprised](status-effects.md#status-surprised), and the action it takes next
does not end that, so your next blow finds it [flat-footed](surprise.md), with
[sneak dice](surprise.md) if you have them. Its second action, like its first, costs it dear, and ends it. A foe quick
enough to act twice before you do has recovered by your blow. Otherwise, "The kobold is not fooled.", and the turn is
spent.

{{#include generated/feinting.md}}

You may feint as often as you like. Half of your feints train Bluff, whatever comes of them. There is
no talking your way out of being [caught stealing](#stealing): a lie is for the fight, or for getting past a
party on the road before it comes to one ([Talk your way past](encounters.md#talking-past)).

## Intimidating

If you are trained in **[Intimidate](skills.md#skill-intimidate)**, **[Intimidate](abilities.md#ability-intimidate)** is in your
[abilities menu](abilities.md): choose it, then a place you see (the [aiming](controls.md#aiming) cursor), and you
threaten whoever stands there. It must be a [creature](creatures.md) hostile to you, within reach, with a heart to
quail: not a skeleton, a spirit, or an animated thing (those without [vitals](combat.md#criticals)). Otherwise it is
refused and takes no time. A threat takes a turn and is spoken aloud: those about you
[hear](hearing.md) a voice.

It is [War cry](abilities.md#ability-war-cry)'s check at one foe. If it hears you and sees you, it rolls 2d12 plus its
[Willpower](stats.md#stat-willpower) [modifier](stats.md#modifiers) against the difficulty below plus your
[Strength](stats.md#stat-strength) modifier plus what your Intimidate adds (the benefits table above); short of it, it
is [frightened](status-effects.md#status-frightened) and runs from you, a few moments a degree. How many degrees
depends on your skill: a little Intimidate frightens a foe for a moment, a great deal as long as a
war cry does. A foe that does not hear or see you pays you no heed, and the turn is spent all
the same.

{{#include generated/intimidating.md}}

You may threaten as often as you like; every threat trains Intimidate, whatever comes of it.

## Treating yourself

If you are trained in **[Heal](skills.md#skill-heal)** ([priests](classes.md#priest), [witches](classes.md#witch), [druids](classes.md#druid), and [paladins](classes.md#paladin) are), **[Heal](abilities.md#ability-heal)** is in your
[abilities menu](abilities.md), under Skills. It treats you: your
**[bleeding](status-effects.md#bleeding)**, your **[poison](status-effects.md#poisoned)**, and a **[disease](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes)** that shows (one you have caught but that has not
shown yet is unknown to you, so you cannot treat it). It treats an [ally](allies.md) of yours
standing beside you the same way, from the start: you know your own allies' hurts, and they are
listed after yours ("Your wolf's bleeding"). With nothing to treat it is greyed out,
"nothing to treat"; with more than one thing, it asks which ("Sickness" is a [disease](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes) you cannot
name yet). The try is yours ([Intelligence](stats.md#stat-intelligence) and your skill); the
difficulty is the patient's. Every try takes about a quarter of a minute, whatever comes of it, and makes no noise.

A try is a check (see [Diseases](diseases.md#checks)): 2d12 plus your [Intelligence](stats.md#stat-intelligence) [modifier](stats.md#modifiers) plus
what your [Heal](skills.md#skill-heal) skill gives (the benefits table above) against the difficulty below; a roll of 2
always fails and a roll of 24 always works.

- **[Bleeding](status-effects.md#bleeding)**: made, you bind the wound and the bleeding stops ("You bind the wound."); failed,
  "The blood still runs.", and you may try again at once, as often as you like. The worse the
  [bleeding](status-effects.md#bleeding), the harder it is: 6 plus 2 for each degree.
- **[Poison](status-effects.md#poisoned)**: made, you draw out some of it: it is a degree weaker ("You draw out some of the
  poison."). Failed, "The poison will not come out."; you may try again at once, as often as you like. 8 plus 1 for each
  degree.
- **A [disease](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes)**: made, you ease it ("You ease the filth fever somewhat."): it counts as one of
  the checks in a row that shake it off, though never the last one, so treating never cures it
  by itself. A [disease](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes) that never goes away by itself, or one a single won check from going,
  instead takes a full interval from your try before it next gets worse. Failed, "You can do no more for the filth fever."; you may try again at once. Its difficulty is the [disease](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes)'s own.

## Fletching

Anyone can make arrows and bolts, after a fashion: **[Fletch](abilities.md#ability-fletch)** is in everyone's [abilities menu](abilities.md),
in [Woodcraft](skills.md#skill-woodcraft)'s place. It takes a knife carried, in your [pack](objects.md#your-pack) or wielded (a dagger or a stiletto
will do, or anything else of the [Daggers and knives](weapon-skills.md#weapon-skill-daggers) skill; a short sword will not), and a bow or a
crossbow [ready](ranged.md#getting-ready), for which it makes [arrows or bolts](ammunition.md) (nothing for a sling). Without them the
menu greys it out, "you have no knife" or "you have no bow or crossbow ready". It uses no material
up, and you can do it anywhere.

Something coming into view, or anything else that would wake you from a [rest](resting.md#how-resting-works), stops the
work: the time is spent and nothing is made. A batch that runs its time rolls a check on your
[Dexterity](stats.md), helped by your Woodcraft, and the check's result decides how much of the count you
get. Under Woodcraft 25 they come out brittle: they fly, but break on any hit. Your Woodcraft earns a
training mark for every finished batch, if you are trained in it. Fletch as often as you like: the
time is the price.

A [ranger](classes.md#ranger)'s [Fletcher](talents.md#talent-fletcher) makes this far quicker and surer, and rangers start with a knife.

## Whittling

With a knife carried, anyone can improve a wooden weapon the way an [anvil](altars-forges-springs.md#forges) improves a metal one: **[Whittle](abilities.md#ability-whittle)** is in everyone's [abilities menu](abilities.md), beside [Fletch](abilities.md#ability-fletch) in [Woodcraft](skills.md#skill-woodcraft)'s place. Choose a weapon, a missile weapon, or a shield whose main part is a wood (bows, crossbows, staves, clubs). Greyed out "you have no knife" without one. An attempt is ten minutes; something coming into view stops it, and then the item is untouched and the attempt does not count. The rules are those of [tempering](altars-forges-springs.md#forges), item for item: a made attempt gives one improvement (Fine, Marvellous, Masterwork) in something the item has, a failed one damages it and takes them all, a damaged item is mended or destroyed, and every attempt is harder than the last, for the item's whole life, so one item has one state however it was worked. The check is 2d12 plus your [Dexterity](stats.md) modifier, what Woodcraft gives, and what talents add to Dexterity checks, against 13 plus 2 for every attempt ever made on the item; the odds are those of the [forge table](altars-forges-springs.md#forges). Woodcraft's second benefit is +8 at 100 and +12 at 200. Your Woodcraft earns a training mark for every finished attempt, if you are trained in it.

## Foraging

If you are trained in **[Survival](skills.md#skill-survival)** ([berserkers](classes.md#berserker), [druids](classes.md#druid), [rangers](classes.md#ranger), and [lizardfolk](races.md#race-lizardfolk) are),
**[Forage](abilities.md#ability-forage)** is in your [abilities menu](abilities.md): choose it, and you spend a while searching
the land around you for something to eat. You can forage only in the wild: on the
[overworld](overworld.md), on open land, in woods, on the hills, or in the marshes, or in the
[local area](overworld.md#local-areas) of such a cell (an area forages as its cell on the [overworld](overworld.md#travel) does). Not on a road, not
in water or on ice, not in a town or a [dungeon](dungeons.md): there the menu greys Forage out ("not usable
here").

A forage takes ten minutes. Something coming into view, or anything else that would wake you
from a [rest](resting.md#how-resting-works), stops it, and a forage cut short finds nothing. One that runs its time finds food
with the land's chance below, plus what your [Survival](skills.md#skill-survival) gives (the benefits table above: up to 30
more); then one of the land's finds is picked by the shares shown, and you gather a few of it
("You gather 2 handfuls of berries."), fresh, into your pack. Otherwise "You find nothing worth
eating." Either way your [Survival](skills.md#skill-survival) may earn a training mark.

{{#include generated/foraging.md}}

What you find is ordinary [food](food.md) (see [Hunger](hunger.md)): berries and mushrooms spoil within days,
hazelnuts keep for months, and an angelica root cures [diseases](diseases.md#how-a-disease-goes). Forage as often as you like, in
the same spot too: the time is the price.
