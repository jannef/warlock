# The Ascmearc

The Ascmearc, "the ash march", is the world you play in. Every new run starts there: **New run**
goes straight on to creating your character (see [Your character](character.md)), and the run
begins on **Beorggeat**, the pass in the mountain wall on the march's western edge, at eight in
the morning: "The pass opens on the Ascmearc. Far to the east, Fyrbeorg smokes." Snow closes
the pass behind you. There is no way back, and nothing to explore there.

It is an old, plain-spoken land of farmers and barrow-diggers: a market town living off the
pass, a cold barrow in the downs, a minster drowned in the fen, and a fire mountain smoking over
it all. How travel, sight, locations, and local areas work on the overworld is on
[The overworld](overworld.md).

## Getting about

The road runs five cells east from the pass to **Cēaptūn**, the market town: under two hours'
walk, and in sight from the pass by day. From the town roads lead on: east over the barrow downs
of Hlāwdūn to the ford over Blæcwæter and the land beyond, south over the bridge on Beorhtburna
and along its bank, and north to the edge of the holly wood. The march is about 120 cells
across, three to four days by road. Rivers can be crossed only at the bridge and the ford.

**Cēaptūn** is the first place to go: a walled market town, safe, with four shops on its
square, the Mynster (the temple, where you may rest and Sister Hild cures and mends for gold),
the Gæsthūs, the inn, where Godgifu has news of the march, and the Gildhalle of the
[Weyfarers' Gilde](guild.md), where Wat One-Eye gives out work (see [Shops](shops.md) and
[People and talk](npcs.md)). You arrive on the square. The town has no stairs: like any outdoor
area, walk off its edge to return to its cell on the overworld, or press `<` when no enemy is
about ([Leaving an area](overworld.md#leaving-an-area)).

Three dungeons lie nearest the town, deeper and more dangerous one after another (see
[Dungeons and danger](dungeons.md)): **Cealdhlāw**, the cold barrow in the downs, which you can
see from the road; **Fennmynster**, the cellars of a minster drowned in the great fen; and
**Fyrdelf**, the fire diggings under the hills below the volcano. The last two are
**hidden**: until a quest tells you where one lies, its cell is just the swamp or the hills it
looks like (see [Hidden locations](overworld.md#hidden-locations)). So are the four lairs of
the criminals the [Weyfarers' Gilde](guild.md#the-wanted) wants dead: Wyrtholm in the fen,
Wulfeshol in Deorcholt, Bānfeld on the downs, and Brocenburg in the holly wood; you are shown
only one of them. The map below shows them all anyway. Places that lead "nowhere yet" are still being built: for now `>` there says so.

## The names

Every name in the Ascmearc is a plain description of what a place is, put into Old English, so
it says what the place is before you get there: a fen minster stands in the fen. The people
have names of the same kind. Creatures keep their plain English names.

| Name | Old English | Meaning | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| the Ascmearc | *asce* "ash, cinders" + *mearc* "march, borderland" | the ash march | the land |
| Beorggeat | *beorg* "mountain" + *geat* "gate, gap" | the mountain gate | the pass where you come in |
| Grægweall | *grǣg* "grey" + *weall* "wall" | the grey wall | the western range |
| Cēaptūn | *cēap* "trade, market" + *tūn* "enclosure, town" | the market town | the town |
| Cēapdenu | *cēap* + *denu* "valley" | the market vale | the town's country |
| Hlāwdūn | *hlāw* "burial mound" + *dūn* "hill, down" | the barrow downs | the hills east of the town |
| Cealdhlāw | *ceald* "cold" + *hlāw* | the cold barrow | the first dungeon |
| Holegnwudu | *holegn* "holly" + *wudu* "wood" | the holly wood | the northern forest |
| Dēopholt | *dēop* "deep" + *holt* "wood, thicket" | the deep holt | its heart |
| Lindwudu | *lind* "lime tree" + *wudu* | the lime wood | the south-western forest |
| Deorcholt | *deorc* "dark" + *holt* | the dark holt | its heart |
| Beorhtburna | *beorht* "bright" + *burna* "stream" | the bright stream | the western river |
| Burnadenu | *burna* + *denu* | the stream vale | its valley and the river road |
| Blæcwæter | *blæc* "black" + *wæter* "water" | the black water | the big river, grey with ash |
| Micelfenn | *micel* "great" + *fenn* "fen, marsh" | the great fen | the marsh |
| Fennmynster | *fenn* + *mynster* "minster, abbey" | the fen minster | the second dungeon |
| Fennhām | *fenn* + *hām* "home, village" | the fen village | the marsh village |
| Ascdūn | *asce* + *dūn* | the ash downs | the hills under the volcano |
| Fyrdelf | *fȳr* "fire" + *gedelf* "digging, mine" | the fire diggings | the third dungeon |
| Fyrbeorg | *fȳr* + *beorg* | the fire mountain | the volcano |
| Wyrtholm | *wyrt* "herb, root" + *holm* "islet" | the herb islet | Mother Sallow's lair |
| Wulfeshol | *wulfes* "wolf's" + *hol* "hole, den" | the wolf's hole | Black Wystan's lair |
| Bānfeld | *bān* "bone" + *feld* "field" | the bone field | Brother Osrick's lair |
| Brocenburg | *brocen* "broken" + *burg* "fort" | the broken fort | Sergeant Herrick's lair |
| Rēcbeorgas | *rēc* "smoke, reek" + *beorgas* "mountains" | the smoking mountains | the volcano's range |
| Fordwīc | *ford* + *wīc* "dwelling, trading place" | the ford village | the village at the ford |
| Ēastmearc | *ēast* + *mearc* | the east march | the eastern grassland |
| Grægsæ | *grǣg* + *sǣ* "sea" | the grey sea | the sea |
| the Mynster | *mynster* | the minster | Cēaptūn's temple |
| the Gæsthūs | *gæst* "guest" + *hūs* "house" | the guest house | Cēaptūn's inn |
| the Gildhalle | Middle English *gild* + *halle* | the guildhall | the guild's hall in Cēaptūn |
| the Weyfarers' Gilde | Middle English *weyfarere* "traveller" + *gilde* | the Wayfarers' Guild | the adventurers' guild |
| Leofric | *lēof* "dear" + *rīc* "ruler" | | the reeve of Cēaptūn |
| Hild | *hild* "battle" | | the sister of the Mynster |
| Wulfrun | *wulf* "wolf" + *rūn* "secret, counsel" | | the mine-factor |
| Godgifu | *god* + *gifu* "gift" | | the innkeeper |

The guild is younger than the town, so its names are Middle English, not Old; its master, Wat
One-Eye, is a commoner, called by a short name and a byname. The guild's ranks (Tin to Gold)
are plain English.

The game writes *ǣ* as **æ** and *ȳ* as **y** (Græg-, Fyr-), as its font draws them.

The language has moved on since the old places were named. People of the march's own day go by
the later forms of the old names, and the outlaws by the bynames the broadsheets give them, in
the English of the day:

| Name | From | Register | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother Sallow, the Fennhām Poisoner | *sallow*, the willow of the fens | a commoner's byname | a wanted poisoner |
| Black Wystan, the Gentleman of the Road | Wystan, from *Wulfstān* "wolf stone" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a wanted highwayman |
| Brother Osrick, the Resurrection Man | Osrick, from *Ōsrīc* "god ruler" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a wanted grave robber |
| Sergeant Herrick, the Deserter | Herrick, from *Hererīc* "army ruler" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a wanted deserter |
| footpad | *foot* + *pad* "road" | Victorian, as the commons say it | a robber on foot |
