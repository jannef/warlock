# Gems

Ten gems turn up in the world, from humble smokestone to diamond. A gem lies loose (`*`, in its
own colour) or is set in an item: a ring, an amulet, a weapon's pommel, a helm's studs. Either
way it is one gem, of a **size** and a **cut**, and what it is worth follows from all three.

## Size and cut

A gem is worth its gem's worth times its size's times its cut's: a large ruby of fine cut is
worth 300 × 15 × 1.6 = 7200 gold, an uncut tiny garnet 60 × 0.3 × 0.25, about 5. Its name says
its size and cut unless they are the plain ones: "a ruby", "a medium amethyst", "a large fine
emerald", and "an uncut tiny garnet" (an uncut gem says so first). Looking at a gem (its letter
in your pack) always says both: "Gem: tiny garnet, uncut". A loose gem weighs what its size says.

Gems of the same kind, size, and cut lie together in one stack ("3 rubies"); any difference makes
another stack.

## Finding gems

Gems are rare. Now and then a find is a loose gem instead of an item: one of the items of loot on
a level's floor, a vault's reward (not one that is sure to be magic), or what a creature drops
where it dies. The showcase's quarry hides a few rubies, and the hub's secret closet a gem or two.

## Items set with a gem

Some items are set with a gem when they are found: rings and amulets often, weapons, shields,
and helms sometimes. The gem is a part of the item (see [Materials](objects.md#materials)), so you
see it at once, identified or not, and the item's name shows it: "a gold and ruby ring", "an
emerald-pommelled long sword", "a garnet-studded iron helm", "a topaz-tipped quarterstaff". A
magic item's prefix goes before it and its suffix after: "a Sharp emerald-pommelled long sword
of the Bear". A rare item keeps its own name, and what it is made of follows: "Grim Bite, an
emerald-pommelled long sword". Looking at the item says the gem's size and cut: "Pommel: small
emerald, fine cut".

A gem adds its worth to its item; it does nothing else yet. A ring set with a gem is drawn in the
gem's colour. Uncut gems are never set in an item.

## Selling gems

General stores buy gems (see [Shops](shops.md)); no shop sells them. A store pays its share of
what the gem is worth, by its kind, size, and cut, and asks that much more for one lying on its
floor.
