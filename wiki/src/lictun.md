# Līctūn

Four cells down the south road from [Cēaptūn](ascmearc.md#cēaptūn), beside the road, is
**Līctūn**, the town's churchyard. A drystone wall runs round it, with a gate on the road. Inside
are rows of grave plots, a yew in each corner, and a little stone chapel in the middle. Nothing
comes there to fight you.

The sexton, **Old Dudda**, walks his rounds of the yard. He will [talk](npcs.md), grimly, but
has nothing to sell and nothing to ask of you. Walk off the yard's edge to leave.

## Your dead

Every character who dies goes into the
[graveyard](saving.md#your-characters-and-the-graveyard), a list kept beside your settings. Each
of them gets a grave of their own in Līctūn, except those who were
[turned to stone](status-list.md#status-stiffening): they stand as
[statues](creature-tricks.md#warning-signs) instead. Look at a grave to read it: "Here lies
Brakka the dwarf warrior, killed by a kobold in Fyrdelf 3, The Ascmearc."

The oldest death is buried first. The yard has forty plots, in four rows of ten, filled from the
top row down and left to right. An empty plot is a kerb of stones round turned earth; a filled
one has its stone.

Ten older stones stand by the chapel. They are the town's own dead, and each has its own words:
"Here lies Ælfric atte Welle, reeve of Cēaptūn, 1361–1419. He kept the peace and the tally
both."

## The crypts

When all forty plots are filled, the flagstone at the end of the path behind the chapel opens
into [stairs](terrain.md#stairs) down to the first crypt. Your dead go on being laid there in the
same order, oldest first, in its niches and stone coffins, filled from the top row down and left
to right. When every place in a crypt is filled, a stair opens in it down to the next. Each crypt
is older and rougher than the one above it, and each has a grave or two of its own, with its own
words.

| Crypt | What it is | Places |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Līctūn crypt 1 | family vaults off a nave | 26 |
| 2. Līctūn crypt 2 | a charnel walled with bones, and a hall of coffins | 30 |
| 3. Līctūn crypt 3 | the priests' crypt, laid out as a cross | 30 |
| 4. Līctūn crypt 4 | the chapmen's gild's round vault | 26 |
| 5. Līctūn crypt 5 | the old sextons' store and workroom | 26 |
| 6. Līctūn crypt 6 | a crypt the ground water flooded | 25 |
| 7. Līctūn crypt 7 | small rooms for the children of a hard winter | 24 |
| 8. Līctūn crypt 8 | a pillared hall whose roof fell in | 31 |
| 9. Līctūn crypt 9 | a pit dug in a year of plague | 26 |
| 10. Līctūn crypt 10 | the first monks' cloister, cut in the rock round their saint's shrine | 32 |
| 11. Līctūn crypt 11 | a hall on columns, built before the English came | 27 |

Nothing lives in the crypts, and they are lit only by a few candles and torches, so bring a
light. Some of the deeper ones stand in water or are half filled with fallen earth and
[rubble](terrain.md), which is slow to cross.
