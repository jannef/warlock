## How to play

You make things. Pick two [trades](#trades) as a new character; each trains a
[craft skill](crafting.md) and comes with its tools. Make, mend, and
[salvage](salvage.md) your own gear, and from level 12 a [patron](patron.md) in Cēaptūn buys your
best work. You are a poor fighter, so start with the easy dungeons and let your work arm you.

## Abilities

A craftsperson works their trades better than anyone, and their class abilities change the
rules of [Crafting](crafting.md). The burgher who buys their best work is on
[The patron](patron.md).

### Trades

A craftsperson learns no craft from the class
itself. Instead they pick two [trades](talents-origins.md#crafts-a-craftspersons-trades) as a new
character, before their other [talents](talents.md): the Talents step asks "Choose a trade"
first. Each trade trains its craft skill at `3d6`, and
[Time served](#talent-time-served) adds 6 to that, so a trade starts at 9 to 24.
Each also comes with the tools of the trade. A craftsperson may spend an ordinary pick on a
third trade too. Their skills grow fast: three [skill rolls](skills.md#improving-a-skill) a level,
and a roll on a trade throws the die one step up.

### Eye for the make

Eye for the make works whenever you look at an item's
description. It adds a line on what [salvage](salvage.md) would give of it if every
check went well, a line on how many more improvements
[the anvil](altars-forges-springs.md#forges) (for metal) or a knife (for wood) could still give
arms or armour, or that the thing is damaged (and, once you have
[Make do and mend](#make-do-and-mend), that your own hands could mend it), and a line when its main material is hard or soft
to [work](crafting.md#which-skill-makes-what). Like [Appraise](skills.md#skill-appraise), it also tells
you how many [enchantments the item can bear](magic-items.md#capacity). It never tells you the
odds, or how often a smith has worked the piece before. With
[Waste not](#waste-not) it counts the three quarters salvage then gives back.

### Waste not

From level 6 a craftsperson has **Waste not**: nothing is lost in their hands but time.

- **Salvage** gives back three quarters of each material's weight instead of half (still at least
  one piece). The hardwood quarterstaff on [Salvage](salvage.md) gives 8 billets instead of 5. A gem's fate is
  unchanged: a failed check still chips it, and a natural 2 still shatters it.
- **A failed recipe** gives back all of its stock and inputs instead of half. A failed batch gives
  back all its wood and metal. A failed [brew](recipes-alchemy.md#how-a-brew-goes) is still spent on its murky draught.
- **A failed temper** at [the anvil](altars-forges-springs.md#forges), or a failed
  [whittle](skill-uses.md#whittling), damages nothing, even on a natural 2: the item stays as it
  was, and a damaged item is never destroyed. The attempt still counts, so the next one is as hard
  as it would have been.

### Patron

The patron ([Patron](#talent-patron), from level 12) is a
well-to-do burgher at the Gæsthūs in Cēaptūn who commissions Fine work from the craftsperson's
trades, one piece at a time, hands over the stock for it, and pays its full worth on delivery.
See [The patron](patron.md).

### Make do and mend

Make do and mend (from level 18) mends a
[damaged](materials.md#item-damage) thing you carry, anywhere, with no anvil and no tools. Choose
it in the [abilities menu](abilities.md#the-abilities-menu), then the item. It takes ten
minutes. If something comes into view, you stop, the item is untouched, and the time is lost.

When the time is up you make one check: 2d12, plus the [modifier](stats.md#modifiers) of the
stat of the [craft that works](crafting.md#which-skill-makes-what) the item's main material (Strength for
metal, Dexterity for wood and leather), plus what that craft skill gives you, against 10 plus
the material's [work](crafting.md#which-skill-makes-what). Untrained in the craft, only the stat counts.
A steel sword is 10 to beat, mithril 14, wood 9.

- **Made:** the damage is gone. The improvements it lost stay lost, and the next attempt at an
  anvil is just as hard as it would have been.
- **Failed:** nothing happens to it. It stays damaged, and you can try again.

It never destroys anything. You cannot mend something you wear or wield (take it off first), a
[protected](materials.md#protected-items) item, goods you have not paid for, or something of a
material no craft works, like cloth or glass. If you are trained in the craft, the check may earn
it a [training mark](skills.md#improving-a-skill).

### Trapwright

Trapwright (from level 25) rigs a trap on the cell beside you: a
tripwire from arrows, stakes from a billet of wood, or a pitfall dug with your pick. A creature
your trap kills is your kill. See [Rigging traps](traps.md#rigging-traps).

### Field forge

Field forge (from level 32) makes an anvil wherever you are: a
[makeshift anvil](altars-forges-springs.md#the-makeshift-anvil) from one ingot and an hour's work.

### Quick hands

From level 40 a craftsperson has **Quick hands**: every craft takes half its time. That is every
[recipe](crafting.md#the-recipes) (an [enchanting](enchanting.md) too), a temper at [the anvil](altars-forges-springs.md#forges), a
[whittle](skill-uses.md#whittling), [cutting and setting a gem](gem-cutting.md),
[salvage](salvage.md), [Make do and mend](#make-do-and-mend),
[Field forge](#field-forge), and [rigging a trap](traps.md#rigging-traps). A long sword takes half as long to forge, and salvage five
minutes instead of ten. A ranger's [Fletcher](class-ranger.md#talent-fletcher) halves arrows too, and
the two together make them in a quarter of the time. Gathering herbs, foraging, scribing, and
deciphering are not crafts and take as long as ever. The times the game shows are the halved
ones.

### Master's hand

From level 50 a craftsperson has **Master's hand**: when a check is made, **4** is added to its
margin before the grade or the cut is read. A failed check still fails, and a natural 2 still
fails or shatters the gem. So a [Fine](crafting.md#how-well-it-comes-out) piece comes from a margin of 2,
Marvellous from 8, and a Masterwork from 16, and every [cut](gem-cutting.md#how-well-it-comes-out)
comes 4 sooner. The grade tables above and the cut tables under
[Gem cutting](gem-cutting.md#how-well-it-comes-out) have a "With Master's hand" column. Setting
a gem, batches, and brews have no grade and are unchanged.

### Maker's mark

From level 60 what a craftsperson makes bears their **mark**. Every weapon, piece of armour,
shield, tool, ring, and amulet they [make](crafting.md#the-recipes) is marked, whatever its
[grade](crafting.md#how-well-it-comes-out), plain ones too. Ingots, batches of arrows and stones, and
[brews](recipes-alchemy.md#how-a-brew-goes) are never marked. Its description says so: "It bears
Brakka's mark." Its name does not change.

- **Worth:** a marked piece is worth **half again** (+50 %, rounded down), on top of its grade. A
  marked Fine steel long sword is worth three times a plain one. A marked piece that is later
  damaged is worth half of that.
- **The patron** pays for that higher worth: a marked piece fetches half again what the same
  piece unmarked does ([The patron](patron.md#delivering)).
- **Enchantments:** a marked piece can bear **one more** ([Capacity](magic-items.md#capacity)).

The mark stays for good: tempering, damage, and mending never take it away.
