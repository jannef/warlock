# Animated objects

Animated objects are things that some will set moving and then left behind: a candlestick that
rocks after you, a teapot that scalds, a broom that scuttles, a rug that closes over you. They go
for the living who come near and leave everything else alone.

- **Household things** are almost harmless, and worth killing for what they are made of. A silver
  candlestick is a prize at any level.
- Deeper, a sword, an axe, or a staff dances alone. This is an
  **[animated blade](creatures.md#creature-animated-blade-5)**: quick and fragile, and its weapon
  is the prize.
- Deeper still walk the composites. **[Animated armour](creatures.md#creature-animated-armour-12)**
  is a suit with nobody inside that fights with its gauntlets. The
  **[animated panoply](creatures.md#creature-animated-panoply-20)** is the full kit of a knight
  with no knight. It [wields and wears](equipment.md#what-fits-where) real
  [magic items](magic-items.md), and their powers are its own. A panoply is among the most
  dangerous things in the game, and among the most rewarding.

## What they are made of

**Household things.** An animated household thing *is* a real household object. An animated
candlestick is a candlestick, of brass, iron, pewter, or (rarely) silver. Its colour on the map is
its material's, and [look mode](controls.md#look-mode) names it: "an animated candlestick of
silver (hostile, unhurt), rocking". A [clatter of cutlery](creatures.md#creature-animated-cutlery)
is three to six pieces of cutlery at once.

**Blades.** An animated blade is one real melee weapon, of a kind found at its level or below. It
**wields** itself as anyone would wield it. It strikes with the weapon's
[damage](combat.md#damage), [threat](combat.md#criticals), and any [magic](magic-items.md) on it (a
flaming blade burns), with the blade's [Strength](stats.md#stat-strength) and a
[rank](weapon-skills.md#raising-a-weapon-skill) in the weapon's skill that grows with its level. The
deeper it is met, the likelier its weapon is magic, and from level 15 it is now and then
[rare](magic-items.md#rarity).

A blade shows on the map as its weapon does: `)` in the colour of the weapon's material. Look mode
names what it wields as it would for anyone: "an animated blade (hostile, unhurt), wielding a steel
long sword". Like any weapon a monster carries, it is
[unidentified](magic-items.md#unidentified-items) until you hold it.

A blade is hard to hit because of its thin edge. It shrugs off thrusts
([piercing](damage-types.md#damage-piercing)) and breaks under heavy blows
([bludgeoning](damage-types.md#damage-bludgeoning)). It floats without a sound.

**Armour and panoplies.** Animated armour and a panoply are made of several real things at once:

- always body armour, a helm, a shield, and gauntlets;
- for a panoply, also a melee weapon, and the deeper it is, boots, a cloak, an amulet, and (from
  level 50) a ring on each hand.

Each piece is drawn as a blade's weapon is, and worn or wielded as anyone would. The armour gives
its [Protection](combat.md#protection), the shield its [Defence](combat.md#defence), the weapon its
blows. A panoply that wields a two-handed weapon carries no shield.

Both show on the map as `A`, in the colour of their body armour's material. Look mode names what
they wear and wield: "an animated armour (hostile, unhurt), wearing a bronze cap and a padded
coat".

Animated armour has no weapon: it slams with its gauntlets. Both are [slow](turns.md#speed) and
[loud](hearing.md#how-loud), and both [open doors](doors.md#creatures-and-doors). Animated armour
shrugs off some of a thrust, which passes through the hollow. A panoply holds a
[rank](weapon-skills.md#raising-a-weapon-skill) in its weapon's skill as a blade does, and with a shield
it has ranks in [Sword and Board](fighting-styles.md#style-sword-and-board).

**One kind each.** All blades count as one kind of creature in every way that matters. You learn
of animated blades once, and a [quest](quests.md) to kill them counts every one. They are drawn
onto a floor as one kind, at the level of the floor's
[danger](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many): a blade of level 12 on a floor of danger 12,
never below level 5 or above 20. All animated armours count as one kind among themselves, and so
do all panoplies.

**When one is destroyed**, half the time what it was made of falls **whole**, as the object it
was (a silver candlestick you can [sell](shops.md#selling)). Otherwise it **breaks into the parts
it was made of**:

- a [gem](gems.md) set in it falls loose;
- every other part falls as [pieces](materials.md#pieces) of its material (a silver candlestick
  as pieces of silver, still worth something);
- paper and wax are lost.

Whatever magic it had is gone with it. They leave no [corpse](food.md#corpses) and never
[bleed](status-effects.md#bleeding).

## What they are

They are constructs. Nothing lives in them to sicken, they have no blood or breath for poison, and
nothing in them bleeds. So they are never **[diseased](status-effects.md#status-diseased)**,
**[poisoned](status-effects.md#status-poisoned)**, or **[bleeding](status-effects.md#bleeding)**.
They can still be [frightened](status-effects.md#status-frightened) (rarely, since their will is
not their own), [held](status-effects.md#status-held),
[staggered](status-effects.md#status-staggered), or
[transfixed](status-effects.md#status-transfixed).

What they are made of decides what hurts them most. Wood and cloth
[burn](damage-types.md#damage-fire), metal takes [lightning](damage-types.md#damage-electricity)
badly, and glass, stone, and bone shatter under heavy blows. Nothing can be
[stolen](abilities.md#ability-steal) from them: what they carry is what they are.

A few have a trick of their own:

- The [animated rug](creatures.md#creature-animated-rug), the rug of smothering, now and then
  closes over what it wraps. You are [engulfed](status-effects.md#status-engulfed) until you
  struggle free or it dies, and it costs you 1 [hit point](hit-points.md#hit-points) a tick. (A
  [charnel heap](creatures.md#creature-charnel-heap)'s costs 2.)
- The [bellows'](creatures.md#creature-animated-bellows) gust may leave you
  [staggered](status-effects.md#status-staggered).
- The [animated lantern](creatures.md#creature-animated-lantern)'s oil bursts as it dies, burning
  everyone beside it ([death throes](creature-powers.md#bursts)). Step back from a dying one.

Blades, like most creatures, come in [grades](creatures.md#grades). One in six is **notched**,
weaker than its kind, and one in six is **keen**, stronger. Look mode says which. No other
animated thing is graded.

## What their magic does for them

Whatever an animated thing wears and wields works for it exactly as it would for you, rings and
amulets too. A Flaming sword [burns](damage-types.md#damage-fire), a ring of the Bear makes its
blows harder, an amulet of the Ox gives it more life, armour of the Salamander takes the edge off
[fire](damage-types.md#damage-fire), and a ring of Light makes it [glow](light.md#what-light-is),
so you see it coming. There are only a few gaps:

| Affix effect | Works for an animated thing? |
|---|---|
| [Stat raises](stats.md) (of the Bear, of the Lynx, of the Ox, ...) | Yes: [hit points](hit-points.md#hit-points), [to hit](combat.md#hitting), [damage](combat.md#damage), [Defence](combat.md#defence) |
| [To hit](combat.md#hitting), [damage](combat.md#damage), added damage, [threat](combat.md#criticals), [criticals](combat.md#criticals), parrying | Yes |
| [Elemental damage](damage-types.md) (Flaming, Freezing, ...) | Yes |
| [Attack speed](turns.md#speed), [moving speed](turns.md#speed) | Yes |
| [Armour](combat.md#defence), shields, Dodge, Deflection, natural armour | Yes |
| [Protection](combat.md#protection) | Yes |
| [Resistances](damage-types.md#armour-and-resistance) | Yes |
| More [hit points](hit-points.md#hit-points) | Yes |
| [Regeneration](healing.md#how-fast-you-heal) | Yes |
| [Stealth](skills.md#skill-stealth) (quieter steps) | Yes |
| [Light](light.md#what-light-is) | Yes: it glows |
| [Skills](skills.md) | Yes, where a rule reads a creature's skill |
| Weight | Its things weigh less, which changes nothing: it carries itself |
| More [mana](mana.md#how-much-mana-you-have), [mana regeneration](mana.md#how-fast-it-comes-back) | Yes, as for any creature (see [Mana](mana.md)), though it casts no spells (yet) |
| [Carrying](burden.md#how-much-you-can-carry) | No, and it needs none |
| Range, draw, durability ([missile weapons](ranged.md#getting-ready)) | No: it wields melee weapons only |

## Where they are found

- The barrow of [Cealdhlāw](ascmearc.md#the-places) keeps candlesticks, cutlery, teapots, and
  cooking pots among its grave goods.
- The drowned minster of [Fennmynster](ascmearc.md#the-places) keeps its candlesticks, books,
  lanterns, and rugs.
- Blades dance in the barrow's lower floors and all through [Fyrdelf](ascmearc.md#the-places),
  where the [duergar smiths'](creatures.md#creature-duergar-smith) work wakes. Empty suits of
  armour walk Fyrdelf's deeper floors (they are of level 12 at least).
- Blades and empty suits also walk [Ūtgang](dungeon-guide.md#ūtgang), the way up from the drake's
  deep.

They never stray anywhere else.

Their homes are two [special rooms](teeming-dens.md#homes-of-animated-things), which may turn up in
any [dungeon](dungeons.md) with a [danger](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many), whatever
lives there:

- An **[armoury](special-rooms.md#room-armoury)** has weapon racks along its walls, with blades,
  hollow armour, and panoplies asleep among them. They are drawn as if the level were four
  [dangers](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many) deeper.
- A **[workshop](special-rooms.md#room-workshop)** has workbenches along its walls, with the
  household things of a house's work asleep about them.

Fyrdelf favours [armouries](special-rooms.md#room-armoury), and on its bottom floor an armoury is
where a panoply stands. No dungeon of the [Ascmearc](ascmearc.md) goes deep enough for a panoply to
walk its halls.

## In numbers

Every animated type's numbers come from its tier and its body, by the formulas below. Each part is
rounded down.

- [Strength](stats.md#stat-strength): 11 + 0.4 × level
- [Toughness](stats.md#stat-toughness): 12 + 0.4 × level
- [Dexterity](stats.md#stat-dexterity): 10 + 0.25 × level
- [Willpower](stats.md#stat-willpower): 16 + 0.35 × level
- [Intelligence](stats.md#stat-intelligence) and [Presence](stats.md#stat-presence): 2 (they are
  mindless)
- then the tier's adjustments to all of these
- [hit points](hit-points.md#hit-points) beyond Toughness: 2.5 × level × the tier's factor
- [damage](combat.md#damage) dice: whichever of 1d2, 1d3, … 5d12 is nearest to 2 + 0.55 × level,
  moved by the tier's steps

A blade has no dice of its own, since it strikes with its weapon. Its [speed](turns.md#speed) is
1200 + 5 × level (at most 1600), and its natural [Defence](combat.md#defence) is 2 + level / 10.

### Blades

A blade's weapon is drawn among the melee weapons found at its level or below, by how common each
is. Then:

- by the first chance below it is made **[at least magic](magic-items.md#rarity)**, and a magic
  one may come out [rare](magic-items.md#rarity), as any find may;
- or by the second chance it is made **[rare](magic-items.md#rarity)** outright;
- otherwise it is plain, of a [material](materials.md#materials) drawn as a monster's gear is.

A magic or rare weapon is made at an [item level](affixes.md#base-items) equal to the blade's.
The blade's [rank](weapon-skills.md#raising-a-weapon-skill) in the weapon's skill is 1 + level / 10, at
most 8.

Animated armour and a panoply draw each of their pieces the same way, slot by slot in the order
below. Each line is tried at its chance, from the level it names, as many times as it says (a ring
for each hand). A slot already taken is skipped, such as the off hand beside a two-handed weapon.
Animated armour's gauntlets strike with the dice of its level, and its
[Strength](stats.md#stat-strength) is the role's. A panoply's rank in
[Sword and Board](fighting-styles.md#style-sword-and-board) beside a shield is level / 15.

### What they leave

For each family: what a creature of it is made of on average (a household thing is its one body),
what of it falls whole under the drop rule, and the chances that each piece is at least
[magic](magic-items.md) and that it is rare. The rest falls as its parts.
